What is this review trying to decide?

Three useful review situations

Situation Main question Useful outcome
Studio crit What should the designer explore or change next? A spatial revision or targeted comparison
Design decision review Is the evidence sufficient to proceed under this brief? Proceed within limits, revise, or seek evidence
Method review Does the method support the stated claim? A justified interpretation, stronger check, or narrower claim

Which decision are you being asked to review today?

These are teaching lenses, not universal professional or journal review procedures. Students advise the author; they do not certify compliance or award marks.

Criterion, metric, threshold, judgment

Keep these four things separate

Term Meaning Pavilion example
Criterion What quality matters, and to whom? A cooler place within a usable walk
Metric What represents that quality, in which conditions? Walk time; modelled UTCI reduction at a stated hour
Threshold Which rule triggers a decision, and who chose it? Walk ≤4 min and reduction ≥4°C
Judgment What action is justified, given the evidence and limits? Advance a candidate provisionally and test its weak assumption

The thresholds are invented project assumptions for this exercise, not standards, regulations or actual student results.

Check the rule as well as the result

  • Relevance: does the metric represent the quality the claim names?
  • Authority: is the threshold from a source, the brief or your assumption?
  • Conditions: are baseline, units, users, location and time comparable?
  • Sensitivity: how far can the rule or input move before the recommendation changes?

A review band is a decision rule. An uncertainty range describes what is uncertain. Label which one you are using.

If the result passes, what have you established—and what is still untested?

Some criteria need qualitative judgment

“The entrance is confusing” needs evidence too.

Criterion: a first-time visitor can recognise the route.

Evidence: sightlines in plan/section, cues at each turn, and an annotated route walkthrough.

Reviewer action: locate the point of confusion and propose a changed view, drawing or user check.

Distinguish a stated user need, an observed problem and your own aesthetic preference. Explain whom a preference serves before treating it as a requirement.

A qualitative criterion still needs an explicit reason and inspectable evidence.

A4 criteria: inspect the argument

Project criteria judge the design options. A4 criteria judge the quality of the argument and workflow.

Criterion · course weight Reviewer question Inspect
Decision clarity · 15% What choice, for whom, between which alternatives? Decision sentence and alternatives
Workflow execution · 20% What changed between runs? One input → operation → output chain
Evidence and uncertainty · 20% What could reverse the conclusion? Metric, threshold source and sensitivity
Design impact · 15% What changes in the design? Drawing, selected option or next action

A4 assessment criteria. These are the course weights; classmates do not award marks here.

A4 criteria: inspect the handoff

Criterion · course weight Reviewer question Inspect
Reproducibility · 15% Can another person reproduce the key result? Inputs, runnable file, instructions and expected output
Revision judgment · 10% What did critique change, and why? One feedback → response → revision link
Communication · 5% What can I understand without narration? Figure/card read silently, then restated

Choose two criteria for detailed review. Always flag an unsupported main claim or missing runnable artifact, even outside your chosen pair.

Choose a useful reviewer action

If you find… Do this Example question
An unclear claim Clarify Which option and user is this recommendation for?
An unsupported step Trace or check Show the input, calculation or source behind this value.
A fragile conclusion Challenge an assumption What happens if this threshold or condition changes?
A claim broader than its evidence Bound the recommendation What can you recommend under the conditions tested?
A consequential gap Specify a next test Which check could change the decision?

Separate an error you can demonstrate from a question you still need to investigate.

Make the comment actionable

“This is unconvincing. Improve the analysis.”

“Your table shows 4.2°C reduction for one modelled condition. The criterion is reliable cooling, but the recommendation covers all summer. Test a contrasting condition or narrow the claim. I can recheck it when the condition and limit are visible beside the result.”

Observation → criterion → consequence → action → completion evidence.

The numbers are illustrative. Point to a specific file, figure or sentence when reviewing real work.

Calibration case: choose a pavilion site

Shared exercise · fabricated data

Brief: walk ≤4 min; modelled UTCI reduction ≥4°C against the same unshaded baseline. All results refer to one assumed summer day at 14:00.

Site Walk time UTCI reduction
A 3 min 3.0°C
B 4 min 4.2°C
C 5 min 5.0°C

Draft claim: “B is the optimal site and will keep all users comfortable throughout summer.”

These are exercise values and provisional targets, not measured student data or comfort standards.

Write a judgment before comparing answers

3 minutes alone · 4 minutes compare · 3 minutes test the change

  1. Restate the claim and identify one relevant criterion.
  2. State what the table supports and what it does not show.
  3. Choose a design-result status and an evidence status independently, using the practice card. Give a reason and scope for each.
  4. Propose one check and name the result that would change your advice.

If you disagree, is it about the criterion, threshold, evidence or acceptable uncertainty?

Keep the next slide hidden until everyone has written. The instructor also writes before comparing. Seek explicit reasons and a useful next check, not identical verdicts. This exercise and the following slide share the 20–30 minute block. The optional massing case at the end can replace the pavilion case in this same slot.

Compare judgments and test one change

Supported: B is the only listed candidate passing both assumed targets in the tested condition.

Unproven: a general optimum, comfort for every user, or performance throughout summer. Reduction alone does not establish the final thermal condition.

Suppose B’s reduction ranges from 3.7–4.4°C across additional test conditions. It no longer passes the 4°C target in every condition.

Next action: identify the condition causing 3.7°C; revise the design, the justified brief, or the scope of the recommendation.

This is a fabricated sensitivity scenario, not a measured confidence interval. If you propose changing the target, retain the original and explain why.

Review the two projects at different depths

Studio massing

Which section or volume changes? Are area and spatial constraints checked on comparable options? Does a geometry screen justify the daylight claim?

Next step: a revised section, comparable option, or narrower claim.

Pavilion research

Are baseline, weather/hour and access assumptions explicit? Does the result survive changing a threshold? What is borrowed method, and what is the contribution?

Next step: a sensitivity check and bounded interpretation.

Both reviewers must point to evidence and justify their advice. You can ask a precise question without mastering the other person’s software.

Prepare your classmate’s review

Session 1 · 30–50 minutes

First 10 minutes: inspect the packet silently. Complete one record on the flipped practice card: rule, expected result, actual observation/location, design judgment, evidence judgment and next check.

Next 10 minutes: clarify terms or files with the author and instructor. Agree the criterion and review question; record any disputed threshold.

“I understand your decision to be . The result supports under . My main question is because ___.”

Choose a check that can be attempted in the 18-minute repair block. Record a larger investigation as a next step.

Practice card: record an actual inspection

Use during the 30–40 minute preparation block

Fill in What to record
Measure or inspect A value + units, a visible feature, source or workflow step
Rule + expectation Target/comparison and source; expected result and conditions
Observation What you actually found, with the exact file/figure/cell/run
Two judgments Does the result meet the rule? Is the evidence adequate?
Next action What to change/check, and what result would resolve the concern

Complete one record. Select both judgments. Point to the evidence. Explain what would change your advice.

A passing result can still need checking

Design result · choose one

  • Meets stated rule
  • Does not meet stated rule
  • Cannot yet judge

Evidence · choose separately

  • Supported within checked scope
  • Problem found
  • Not established

An option can fail a target while the analysis is sound. A passing number can rest on evidence you have not checked.

B: 4.2°C ≥ 4°C. The table comparison passes for the recorded condition. The underlying model is unverified; a summer-wide comfort claim needs more evidence.

Illustrative pavilion case. Give a reason and scope with each judgment. “Cannot yet judge” does not mean a demonstrated failure.

Session 2: review, act, recheck

50 minutes · two students and the instructor

Time Activity Save
0–12 B leads the review of A’s work Review card + author’s response
12–24 A leads the review of B’s work Review card + author’s response
24–42 Each author performs one check or revision Revised result, or exact blocker
42–47 Peer recheck: 2½ minutes per project Supported claim and remaining gap
47–50 Record the response and next action Dated A4 ledger entry

The instructor checks evidence, clarifies disagreements and supports both repairs. Each student acts as reviewer and author.

Each review takes 12 minutes

Time Lead Action
0–3 Reviewer Read back the claim; locate evidence; explain one challenge
3–5 Author Correct misunderstandings and supply context
5–8 Both Inspect one link or discuss the proposed test
8–10 Reviewer Recommend an action and completion evidence
10–12 Author Accept, adapt, defer or reject the advice, with a reason

End with a defensible next action, not a list of preferences.

For one student, the instructor supplies an example packet and takes the second role. For three students, use three eight-minute reviews in the same 24-minute block: two-minute readback, one-minute clarification, two-minute challenge, two-minute advice, one-minute author response. Preserve the repair block; split the five-minute peer recheck into about 100 seconds per project.

The author decides how to respond

Response What to record
Accept The advice fits; I will make this change.
Adapt The concern is valid; this other check addresses it better.
Defer It needs more time/data; the claim stays qualified or pending.
Reject The advice does not fit the evidence or scope; here is why.

Locate the disagreement. Preserve the concern and your reasoning, including advice you did not follow.

This contributes to the existing A4 Feedback + Revision Ledger. It is not an additional graded assignment.

Prioritise the consequence of the gap

Priority Why it matters Example
Critical Could reverse or invalidate the main recommendation Wrong units, missing input, unsupported threshold
Important Limits confidence or who can use the result Untested condition, unclear method or source
Polish Improves readability without changing the conclusion Label alignment or consistent colours

Which fix most changes what someone is justified in doing with your work?

Explain the priority for this project. A missing unit can be a critical error, not a cosmetic label problem.

Perform one check or revision

Session 2 · 18-minute repair block

Before starting, write: **I expect ; if I observe , I will change ___.**

  • Claim/figure route: trace a value, correct a label, narrow a claim, or draw the missing comparison.
  • Existing tool route: rerun one case, change one input, or test a GH/Python component against a known result.

Save the input, action, observed result and judgment. Keep the check small enough to finish or diagnose here.

If blocked, save the exact failing step and next check. A proposed test is not a completed verification.

Recheck the changed work

2½ minutes per project

Inspect the revised artifact and saved check, alongside the author’s explanation.

  1. What changed in the claim, evidence or rule?
  2. Does the recorded result address the original concern?
  3. What is supported now, and what remains uncertain?

“I accepted/adapted/deferred/rejected ___ because . I changed . The check showed ; remains unresolved.”

Save one dated ledger entry. A4 requires at least three entries across the project; today’s entry contributes to that total.

Reconstruct your review in four steps

Object: your judgment as reviewer and your response as author.

Did Which claim did I inspect, and what did I advise?

Assumed Which criterion or threshold did my advice depend on?

Risk What was missing, or what could change my judgment?

Verify What did the check establish, and what is pending?
