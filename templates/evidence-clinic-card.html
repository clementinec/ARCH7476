Week 11: Project Evidence Clinic Card

Compare your abstract-based interpretation with the paper, then revise your own project

Use this with the Week 11 slides. All four students undertake the same task. Your claim may concern a PhD research idea, a method choice or an undergraduate studio decision. Choose one consequential claim already in your work. This is a formative clinic feeding A4, with no new graded submission.

Preparation

Bring the project claim, a relevant paper’s abstract and full text where accessible, and the paragraph, drawing or output that currently relies on it. Share or open the material locally. A RIS export and API key are optional; neither is necessary for the source-reading task.

If the full paper is inaccessible, record the support as unverified, with the section or evidence you need. Do not equate access failure with irrelevance. The shared article below can demonstrate the reading process while your own project claim remains qualified.

Brainstorm and Pitch

Spend eight minutes writing:

  • What do I want to investigate, choose or change in my project?
  • What must be true for that decision to make sense?
  • What evidence would support or challenge it?
  • What would change if the claim did not hold?

Each student then has 90 seconds to show their idea and evidence need, followed by 60 seconds for one peer/instructor question. Four pitches take ten minutes.

Your Before-and-After Record

Keep the initial interpretation. Do not overwrite it after reading the rest of the paper.

Field Your record
Project / author / date ___
Current decision and claim I propose ___ because I believe ___.
What would support or challenge it? I would need to observe/establish ___.
Source and access Citation + DOI/URL: . Abstract / full article / supplement inspected: .
Abstract-only interpretation The abstract reports . I inferred for my project.
Intended action before checking I was going to choose/test/change ___ because ___.
Methods and conditions What was studied, where, with which inputs/sample, comparison and controls? Location: ___.
Actual result Measure, unit, comparison and finding: . Table/figure/section/page: .
Limitation relevant to my use The source states ___ / I cannot establish . Location or missing item: .
Transfer to my project My site, users, data, scale or method differs in . This matters because .
Judgment and reason Supported as stated / needs narrowing or adaptation / contradicted by inspected evidence / not yet established: ___.
Revised claim I can justify ___ under ; remains unresolved.
Project consequence Change / retain / defer this design or research decision: , because .
Next test Check ; expected observation ; change my decision if ___.
Peer recheck Reviewer ___ could trace ; still questioned .

These judgment labels are course working terms, not publication recommendations. A missing detail in an abstract is not proof that the study is defective. Inspect the article before attributing an error to its authors. Full-text reading can support retaining your original claim; explain that result honestly.

Twenty-Two-Minute Comparison

  1. 4 minutes: read the abstract and save your initial interpretation/action.
  2. 8 minutes: inspect the relevant methods, results and limitations. Locate the evidence; this is a focused check, not a claim to have appraised the whole paper.
  3. 6 minutes: exchange findings with a partner, three minutes per student.
  4. 4 minutes: write the revised claim, consequence for your project and remaining test.

A source can establish a narrower point than the one your project needs. For instance, check whether the output is a proxy for your desired outcome, whether a simulation has been validated in a relevant setting, and whether the reported comparison supports causal or broader claims. These are inspection questions, not assumptions that every paper has these problems.

Shared Article for Demonstration

Mora & Bardhan (2025), Towards carbon neutrality: mapping mass retrofit opportunities in Cambridge, UK. HTML abstract and full text · DOI.

Read the abstract first, then §3.1.3 and §4.4. Ask which sampling and operating-condition details change your intended use of thermal imaging. The slide example identifies those details; record your own inference before revealing it. This article demonstrates the reading method and does not supply evidence for an unrelated project.

Four-Minute Clinic and Project Revision

Each author has two minutes to show the before/after claim and source evidence. Peers and the instructor then have two minutes to challenge a transfer assumption and identify a feasible revision. Four students take 16 minutes.

Use the next 20 minutes to revise a hypothesis, method choice, design option, claim or small test. Record whether you accept, adapt, defer or reject the feedback, and why. If you attempt a test, keep the actual result or exact blocker. A proposed test remains planned.

Spend eight minutes on pair rechecks, four minutes per project. The reviewer opens the evidence location and tests whether the revised interpretation and project consequence follow. Use the final six minutes to save this card, the changed artifact and an A4 ledger entry.

Supporting Tools: Search and Regex

Tools help locate and organise evidence. The reading and project judgment remain the main activity.

Where Example Limit to inspect
Google "courtyard ventilation" site:edu filetype:pdf -hotel Restricting source/file type or excluding a word can hide relevant material.
OpenAlex "courtyard ventilation" AND housing A search hit is a candidate, not proof of relevance or quality.
Regex over text \bshade\b with i Misses synonyms and matches words even in a negative statement.

Google’s search operators and database query languages differ from regex. Check the syntax for the specific tool rather than pasting the same expression everywhere. Google operator guide · OpenAlex search guide.

Regex means regular expression: a character-pattern rule. In the local Regex Lab, \b marks a word boundary, (a|b) chooses alternatives, [0-9]{2} means two digits, and \. matches a literal full stop. The i flag ignores case; m makes ^ and $ work at line boundaries. Syntax reference.

For example, \b(shade|shading)\b with i matches SHADE and shading, but misses shaded and overhang. It also matches “no shade benefit”; a word match does not establish a positive finding.

Try one preset, predict a match and a miss, then inspect the output. The filename pattern checks names; the DOI-field pattern extracts text. Neither establishes valid research evidence. The examples are explicitly marked as format/pattern demonstrations.

What RIS and an Abstract Can Supply

RIS is a tagged citation format. ScienceDirect offers RIS export; availability of an abstract depends on the export path and options. Keep the untouched export and inspect its fields. A record may contain title, authors and DOI without an abstract, and an abstract is not the methods/results text. Official export instructions.

Use a parser for complete records, repeated author fields and continued lines. Apply regex only to a defined cleanup or extraction and inspect its effect. A well-formatted record can still support an unsuitable inference.

OpenAlex Setup

An API lets a program request data from a service. An API key identifies your account for access and usage limits.

  1. Create your own OpenAlex account.
  2. Verify your email, then open Settings → API key.
  3. Store the key privately, such as an environment variable. Do not paste it into slides, shared notebooks, screenshots or a GitHub repository.

Checked 24 November 2026: basic requests can run without a key. A free verified key increases the request budget and enables cursor paging beyond the first page. If setup fails, use the article’s DOI/HTML page and continue the evidence clinic. Official authentication instructions.

For students already using a terminal with OPENALEX_API_KEY set privately, this is a single DOI lookup:

curl --fail-with-body \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ${OPENALEX_API_KEY}" \
  'https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241337'

Compare the returned title/authors/year with the source. Save the OpenAlex ID beside the original DOI, retrieval date and discrepancies. Title search can suggest candidates when the DOI is missing; inspect them before accepting a match. Lookup documentation.

OpenAlex’s abstract_inverted_index stores words with their positions; reconstruction requires ordering those positions. It can be absent. Preserve missing, and check the article rather than inventing abstract text. Field definition.

Why Abstract-Only Automated Analysis Can Mislead

An LLM or classifier cannot inspect evidence absent from its input. Distinguish a source’s reported result from the model’s inference about your project. Require a source locator for consequential claims and mark missing conditions as not reported in the supplied text.

Check the actual article for measurement definitions, sample and controls, comparisons, numerical results and limitations. Record the difference between the source context and yours. More text can make assessment possible, but it does not remove the need to judge study quality and transferability.

Optional Literature-Set Extension

If you extend the RIS/OpenAlex demonstration, retain the query, dates, filters, source-specific counts, raw export, duplicate decisions and screening reasons. Label a classroom subset as a subset. Matching the same records in OpenAlex is metadata checking; an independent search that discovers additional records needs its own log.

These records can support PRISMA reporting, but a cleaned bibliography does not complete screening, retrieval, eligibility assessment or synthesis. Do not fill unperformed flow-diagram stages with zero. PRISMA search guidance · flow diagram guidance.

Carry the Result into A4

Update the relevant Practice Case, Research Brief or Object Card. Save source locations and verification notes in the capsule alongside the runnable workflow, inputs and instructions. Screenshots can support the explanation but cannot replace the runnable artifact.

Use the existing Feedback + Revision Ledger to record what the peer/instructor challenged, what changed and why. A4 still requires at least three entries across the project and a Reflection Note. See the six-component brief and final checklist.
