Week 10: Flipped Review Practice Card

Record What You Inspected, Judge the Result, Explain the Next Action

This is a formative working note, not another graded submission. Reviewers do not award marks. Everyone first uses a real paper in the shared 10-minute calibration exercise in Session 1. Then choose a Research or Studio focus for reviewing your classmate’s own A3/A4 work in Session 2. Save the record locally or share it through Slack.

Choose One Track

These are course-curated questions, not an official journal or conference review form. The real review forms explain their own criteria and recommendation scales; this exercise does not ask you to issue a publication decision from an extract.

Research: Review Your Own Workflow and Result

Task: use the real paper case in the paper track to practise locating a claim and checking its evidence during the shared calibration. Then transfer those questions to one claim, workflow and result from your own A3/A4 project; your classmate reviews that work.

Questions to answer:

  • What does your project claim to add or establish, and what comparison supports that contribution?
  • How is your metric defined? What are its units, baseline, sample and test conditions?
  • What value or observation did your workflow actually produce, and where is it recorded?
  • Does the method support your claim? What validation was completed, and which limitation affects the result or contribution?
  • What specific clarification or revision is needed, and what response would change your judgment?

Evidence: your actual metric definitions, workflow, outputs, validation records and relevant sources. Record missing information; do not substitute an invented value or acceptance cutoff. Use the published paper as a model for the questions, not as a substitute for your own project evidence.

Output: one inspection record for your classmate’s project, with precise locations and a judgment limited to the evidence checked; one required revision or check; then a saved result or blocker and a dated feedback-ledger entry. The shared paper exercise produces separate calibration notes, not a whole-paper acceptance or rejection decision.

Studio: Review One Decision in an Actual Project

Task: select one of the studio question groups: site/route, spatial/structural coherence, environment, or character/detail. Bring a plan, section, detail or dataset from your own project; your classmate reviews it.

Questions to answer:

  • What quality matters here, to whom, and why?
  • Where can you see the relevant spatial relationship, load path, environmental result or material/detail decision?
  • Which drawing or data supports the claim? What remains hidden or inconsistent?
  • What consequence does this have for use, space, construction or the intended architectural character?
  • Which revision or comparison would resolve the concern, and what would you look for in the revised work?

Evidence: the actual project material and its brief, sources or stated design intentions. Use a numerical threshold only when it is relevant and justified; qualitative spatial judgments need reasons and located evidence, not a universal cutoff.

Output: one completed inspection record, an annotated observation with one consequence, and one proposed revision plus the evidence needed to recheck it.

Before Class

Allow 15–20 minutes to share your packet and read the other student’s work. Share early enough for your classmate to inspect it.

For both tracks, share a claim or decision from your own A3/A4 work, its actual drawing or result, the criterion/metric/comparison and its source, one limitation and one review question. Identify missing material honestly. A full A4 package is not required yet. The published paper is the shared Session 1 calibration material; it does not replace this project packet.

You may use a separate copy during the 10-minute paper calibration, marked with the paper citation and sections inspected; do not apply course weights or issue a publication verdict from that extract. Repeat the project record for a second claim if time allows; you are not expected to rerun the entire project.

What Exactly Should You Inspect?

Choose the row that fits the claim. You do not need to perform every kind of check.

Claim or artifact Measure or look for What an adequate check records
An option meets a project target Area, walk time, model output or another relevant value; units, scenario and target source Observed value versus the rule, margin where meaningful, and which conditions were checked
One option performs better Comparable alternatives, common baseline, fixed constraints and the criterion used to rank them The difference under like-for-like conditions; any trade-off that prevents a general “best” claim
A paper’s result supports its contribution The claimed contribution, metric definition, actual reported values, baseline and study conditions Where the result supports the claim; a validity/contribution limitation and the clarification that would change your judgment
A spatial quality is improved A specific sightline, route, section, sequence or other visible feature Annotated evidence tied to the stated user need; remaining ambiguity or a proposed observation/user check
A workflow result can be trusted One input → operation → output; manual calculation, known case or rerun where feasible Expected versus observed result, units, tolerance and its justification; exact blocker if not completed
A conclusion follows from a figure The claim’s users, locations and times versus the evidence’s actual coverage A claim limited to what was checked, with the important caveat visible

A qualitative criterion needs inspectable evidence too; do not invent a numerical score for everything. A review band is a decision rule, not automatically an uncertainty interval. A result close to a threshold needs its margin stated; closeness alone does not create a new pass/fail rule.

Judge the Result or Design and Evidence Separately

1. Does a stated target or comparison rule apply?

Use the first two statuses only when the claim has an applicable rule. For a qualitative interpretation or contribution claim without such a rule, use Not applicable, state your finding in words, and assess its evidential support below. Do not manufacture a pass/fail threshold.

Status Use it when…
Meets stated rule The observed value or feature satisfies the declared rule under the recorded conditions. Name those conditions.
Does not meet stated rule The observation breaches the declared rule. State the gap or missing feature.
Cannot yet judge A necessary rule, observation, unit or condition is missing, disputed or unresolved. State what is needed.
Not applicable This claim does not assert that a target or comparison rule is met. Record the qualitative finding and its reason instead.

If a range represents tested conditions, identify which pass and fail. If it represents uncertainty around an estimate, crossing the threshold leaves the judgment unresolved unless a justified decision rule says otherwise. In either case, do not claim the rule is met throughout a range that crosses it. Passing one criterion does not establish that all project constraints are satisfied or that an option is best.

2. Is the evidence adequate for this conclusion?

Status Use it when…
Supported within checked scope Your inspection supports this particular conclusion. Name the check and its limits.
Problem found You can point to an error, mismatch, unsupported extension of the claim or contradictory result.
Not established A necessary source, run or verification has not been supplied or inspected. This is an open question, not proof of an error.

You may need different evidence statuses for different layers: a table comparison can be checked while the underlying simulation remains unverified.

What Counts as Good, Acceptable or Needing Work?

These are judgments about the work and the next action, not marks for the student.

What you find Defensible judgment Next action
The option meets the relevant rules and the claim is supported by the checks made Acceptable to advance within the stated scope. Call it stronger than another option only with a justified comparison. State the conditions and remaining limits; proceed to the next design step.
The option misses a rule, but the analysis demonstrating that failure is sound The design needs revision; the analysis is useful. Revise/reject the option or investigate a justified change to the brief.
A number passes, but the needed evidence has not been checked A provisional result, not yet an established recommendation. Hold the broader claim and perform the missing check.
A demonstrated error or overclaim affects the recommendation The current conclusion needs correction. Correct the issue, rerun/reinterpret as needed and recheck the claim.
No design target applies, but the inspected paper claim follows from its methods/results within stated limits The inspected claim is defensible; this is not a whole-paper publication verdict. Explain the supported contribution and any remaining question.

If a necessary rule or observation is absent, say “cannot yet judge”. If the claim does not require a target/comparison rule, use “not applicable” and assess the argument in words. Explain what would resolve uncertainty rather than labelling the paper or project “bad.”

A Comment Tied to a Paper’s Evidence

This is a course-authored sentence pattern, not a quote from a reviewer or a finding about a particular paper. Fill the brackets only after inspecting the selected paper.

Table [identifier] reports [actual metric and value] against [baseline] under [conditions]. This supports [bounded claim]. The statement in [section] also covers [untested condition or outcome]. Please provide [specific clarification/check] or narrow that statement. I would change my judgment if [the evidence needed] were available.

Use this pattern only when you find that mismatch. If the paper already bounds its claim appropriately, state the strength and ask a different question supported by your inspection. A paper’s reported metric, a venue’s reviewer rating and a course grade are different things; consult the real review forms for the venue-specific task.

Say Your Review Clearly

I inspected **___** in [artifact/location]. For question/comparison , I observed . My result/design finding is . The evidence , because I checked **___** but not . My advice is . I would change that advice if **___**.

The Existing A4 Criteria

Project criteria judge the design options. A4 criteria judge the quality of the argument and workflow.

Criterion Course weight Useful reviewer action
Decision clarity 15% Restate the decision and alternatives
Generative workflow execution 20% Trace one input → operation → output
Evidence quality and uncertainty 20% Inspect the metric, threshold source and a sensitivity
Design impact 15% Find the actual design action
Reproducibility 15% Locate runnable files, instructions and expected result
Feedback/revision judgment 10% Inspect the reason for accepting or declining advice
Documentation and communication clarity 5% Read silently and report what you understood

The weights come from the A4 brief. They explain the course assessment; they are not a peer-scoring exercise or a publication rubric. For A4 work, select two criteria for detailed review, while always flagging an unsupported main claim or missing runnable artifact.

Twelve-Minute Review

Time Lead Action
0–3 Reviewer Read back the claim, locate evidence and explain one challenge
3–5 Author Correct misunderstandings and supply context
5–8 Both Inspect one link or discuss the proposed test
8–10 Reviewer Recommend an action and completion evidence
10–12 Author Accept, adapt, defer or reject the advice, with a reason

Both tracks use this protocol for the student’s own A3/A4 work. The author responds to feedback on their actual project, then performs one check or revision in the repair block. The published-paper calibration is a separate Session 1 exercise.
