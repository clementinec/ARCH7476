Assignment 2: Evidence and Pathway Map

Choose the Sources and Workflow That Fit Your Next Decision

Due: End of Week 6
Weight: 20% of final grade
Length: 4-5 pages + evidence/pathway matrix
Expected effort: roughly 8-12 hours outside class
Format: PDF submission via course portal

Assignment Overview

A2 turns the next decision from A1 into a workable plan. You will map the evidence landscape around that question, compare the relevant tools, and choose one primary pathway for the rest of the semester:

  • spreadsheet
  • GIS
  • Python

This is the point where pathway choice becomes explicit.

Learning Objectives

By completing A2, you will be able to:

  1. Identify the most relevant evidence sources for your question
  2. Evaluate whether those sources are actually accessible and useful
  3. Compare pathways based on methodological fit, not fashion
  4. Choose one primary workflow with a realistic scope
  5. Define the inputs, outputs, and limits of that workflow

Core Deliverables

Part 1: Decision Context Recap (about 0.5 page)

Briefly restate:

  • the upcoming design decision
  • the stakeholder or audience
  • the specific claim or comparison you want to support
  • why the decision matters now

Part 2: Evidence Landscape (about 1.5-2 pages)

Identify and categorize the evidence relevant to your question.

Possible categories:

  • precedents and case studies
  • standards and codes
  • published research
  • site observations
  • government or open datasets
  • surveys or interviews
  • simulation outputs
  • spatial layers and maps
  • your own design variants and option studies

For each category, explain:

  • what it can tell you
  • what it cannot tell you
  • how trustworthy it is
  • how easy or hard it is to access

Part 3: Pathway Comparison (about 1-1.5 pages)

Compare the three semester pathways for your question, even if one is obviously best.

Address:

  • what spreadsheet would let you do
  • what GIS would let you do
  • what Python would let you do
  • why one pathway is the best fit right now
  • what the chosen pathway still cannot solve on its own

If you choose GIS, be specific about whether the workflow will lean more on:

  • QGIS
  • ArcGIS
  • geopandas

The course expectation is that code-based GIS references should sit concretely in geopandas, not in generic “maybe I will code later” language.

Part 4: Primary Pathway Selection (about 1 page)

Commit to one primary pathway and describe the workflow you expect to build.

Define:

  • likely input files or sources
  • likely intermediate steps
  • expected outputs
  • what a minimal successful pilot would look like
  • what may fail or slow you down
  • one note confirming your pathway “hello world” has already run — a sample notebook rerun, a QGIS layer loaded, or a workbook structured; nobody should enter Week 7 never having opened their tool

Part 5: Evidence and Pathway Matrix

Create a visual matrix with rows for evidence sources and columns such as:

  • source type
  • relevance to the decision
  • access level
  • quality / reliability
  • best supporting pathway
  • likely role in A3 / A4

Use symbols or color coding to show:

  • high vs low relevance
  • easy vs hard access
  • strong vs weak confidence

Differentiated Expectations

Undergraduate

  • narrower source set
  • cleaner pathway choice
  • strong emphasis on feasibility and clarity

MArch

  • stronger stakeholder logic
  • pathway choice tied more directly to practice or studio use
  • better judgment about what evidence is decision-relevant

PhD

  • deeper method critique
  • more explicit treatment of limitations, transferability, and uncertainty
  • clearer rationale for why the chosen workflow is analytically defensible

Assessment Criteria

Evidence Mapping Quality (35%)

  • Are the important evidence categories identified?
  • Is relevance separated from mere availability?
  • Are gaps and weak spots named clearly?

Pathway Reasoning (35%)

  • Is the chosen pathway justified against the alternatives?
  • Does the student select a workflow that fits the question?
  • If GIS is chosen, is the role of QGIS, ArcGIS, or geopandas stated clearly?

Feasibility and Scope (20%)

  • Can the proposed workflow realistically be prototyped in A3?
  • Are likely failure points acknowledged?
  • Is the student avoiding tool overreach?

Communication Quality (10%)

  • Is the matrix legible and useful?
  • Is the writing structured and decision-oriented?

Common Failure Modes

  • choosing the most technical pathway without a real need
  • confusing software awareness with workflow design
  • failing to say what the output will actually be
  • choosing GIS without naming whether the work is GUI-based or geopandas-based

What a Strong Submission Looks Like

A strong A2 submission makes a hard choice. It shows why one pathway is fit for purpose, what evidence it will rely on, and how the student will turn that pathway into a workable prototype in A3.
