Assignment 1: Evidence Audit + Next Decision Brief

From a Prior Design Decision to the Evidence You Need Next

Due: End of Week 2
Weight: 15% of final grade
Length: 3-4 pages + 1 visual audit sheet
Expected effort: roughly 6-9 hours outside class
Format: PDF submission via course portal

Assignment Overview

This first assignment replaces the old generic decision-claim brief. The goal is to start from a design decision you already made, inspect what actually counted as evidence at the time, and then pivot toward the next studio decision you expect to face.

You are not being asked to pretend you already worked scientifically. You are being asked to make your existing decision process visible and then improve it.

Learning Objectives

By completing A1, you will be able to:

  1. Describe a prior design decision in concrete terms
  2. Distinguish intuition, precedent, assumption, and evidence
  3. Audit where confidence was justified and where it was overstated
  4. Define a new decision that would benefit from stronger evidence
  5. Identify which pathway might support that next decision

Week 1 In-Class Milestone

Week 1 is not only an introduction to the assignment. It is the first working session for A1.

Students should leave that class with:

  • one prior decision selected
  • one rough inventory of what counted as evidence at the time
  • one weak link or missing evidence gap identified
  • one upcoming studio decision drafted

Use that material as the starting draft for the submission rather than beginning from scratch later.

Core Deliverables

Part 1: Prior Decision Description (about 1 page)

Choose one decision you already made in studio, research, or practice. It should be specific enough that someone could understand what alternatives existed.

Decisions from your current thesis or studio project are explicitly welcome — the course is designed to run alongside them, and the strongest A4 packages usually feed a live project.

Address:

  • what the design decision was
  • when it was made
  • what alternatives were in play
  • who the decision was for
  • what constraints mattered most

Examples:

  • choosing a facade proportion
  • locating an entrance
  • deciding between two site strategies
  • selecting a shading or glazing approach
  • choosing a circulation layout or public-space arrangement

Part 2: Evidence Audit (about 1-1.5 pages)

Break down what informed that decision at the time.

For each input, explain:

  • what it was
  • where it came from
  • whether it was measured, observed, simulated, cited, assumed, or inferred — or tested by making (a variant, sketch series, or mockup you produced in order to find out)
  • how strongly it really supported the final move

Your audit should identify:

  • evidence that was genuinely useful
  • evidence that was weak but treated as strong
  • gaps that you ignored or could not address
  • places where aesthetic judgment or convenience filled the gap

Part 3: Confidence and Limits (about 0.5-1 page)

Reflect on the quality of the earlier decision.

Address:

  • what you would still defend
  • what you would now question
  • what evidence would have changed the decision
  • whether the decision was reversible, risky, or consequential

Part 4: Next Decision Brief (about 1 page)

Now pivot to an upcoming studio or design question.

Define:

  • the next decision you expect to make
  • the main stakeholder or audience
  • why evidence would help
  • what kind of evidence would matter most
  • which of the three course pathways seems most promising right now: spreadsheet, GIS, or Python

You are not locking the pathway yet. You are making an early judgment that A2 will test.

Part 5: Visual Audit Sheet

Create one visual page that shows:

  • the original design decision
  • the evidence types that informed it
  • the weak links or missing evidence
  • the next decision you now want to investigate

Acceptable formats:

  • annotated diagrams
  • a table with color coding
  • a process map
  • a one-page evidence matrix

Differentiated Expectations

Undergraduate

  • choose a tightly bounded decision
  • be explicit about uncertainty even if the evidence is simple
  • use clear categories and straightforward reflection

MArch

  • connect the decision to a real design or stakeholder context
  • show stronger judgment about what evidence would actually change practice
  • articulate why the next decision matters in studio or professional terms

PhD

  • treat the audit as a method critique, not only a reflection
  • distinguish evidence quality more sharply
  • define a next decision that can support a more rigorous investigation

Assessment Criteria

Specificity and Honesty (35%)

  • Is the prior decision concrete and understandable?
  • Does the audit avoid hindsight theatrics and describe the process honestly?
  • Are evidence and assumption clearly separated?

Quality of Reflection (30%)

  • Does the student identify where confidence exceeded the evidence?
  • Are missing inputs and weak links clearly named?
  • Is the critique useful rather than vague?

Strength of the Next Decision Brief (25%)

  • Is the next decision specific and relevant?
  • Is the case for needing evidence convincing?
  • Is the tentative pathway choice plausible?

Communication Quality (10%)

  • Is the writing clear and structured?
  • Does the visual audit sheet help rather than decorate?

Common Failure Modes

  • choosing a decision that is too broad to audit
  • pretending precedent images alone were sufficient evidence
  • using generic statements like “more data is needed” without specifying what data
  • treating software choice as the decision instead of the evidence problem

What a Strong Submission Looks Like

A strong A1 submission makes one real decision legible, admits where the earlier reasoning was weak, and sets up a next question that the rest of the semester can answer through a clearly scoped evidence pathway.
