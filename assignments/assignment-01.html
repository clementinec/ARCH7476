Assignment 1: Total Recall

Reconstruct the Evidence Behind a Past Studio Decision

Due: End of Week 3
Weight: 15%
Format: PDF submission with one visual case board

Purpose

A1 asks you to return to one past studio decision and reconstruct how you built a case for it.

You are not being asked to prove that the old decision was right. You are being asked to identify what evidence actually supported it, what was assumption or preference, and what kind of workflow could have made the decision stronger.

Core Question

What evidence did I really use, and what evidence workflow did I wish I had?

Required Project Basis

Use a past studio project or a completed design decision from studio, research, or practice.

This retrospective basis matters because the decision is stable enough to audit honestly.

Session 2 Check-Ins That Feed A1

These check-ins are formative. They help you build the final submission and should be reflected in your board and short audit.

Week Bring to Session 2 Round-table question A1 component
1 one image, diagram, or board fragment from the past project What decision is visible here, and what evidence did you think supported it? Prior Studio Decision + Evidence Recall
2 the same decision with evidence, precedent, assumption, and uncertainty marked What does this evidence actually support, and what does it not show yet? Evidence Critique
3 rough table, chart, matrix, or board draft What variants or scenarios could be compared, and what would make the trade-off visible? Total Recall Board + transition to A2

Use the Project Reasoning Check-In template if you want a consistent note structure.

Deliverables

1. Prior Studio Decision

Describe one specific decision.

Include:

  • project context
  • design decision
  • alternatives considered
  • audience or critic
  • constraints that shaped the decision

Examples:

  • facade proportion
  • entrance location
  • circulation route
  • site strategy
  • shading or glazing decision
  • public-space arrangement
  • massing or orientation choice

2. Evidence Recall

List what informed the decision at the time.

Sort each input as one of:

  • measured evidence
  • observed evidence
  • simulated evidence
  • standard or code
  • precedent
  • tutor/client/stakeholder input
  • assumption
  • intuition or taste

3. Evidence Critique

Explain:

  • what the evidence actually supported
  • what was treated as stronger than it really was
  • what was missing
  • where confidence exceeded evidence
  • what could have changed the decision

4. Missing Workflow

Identify a possible workflow that would have improved the decision.

This may involve:

  • GH / parametric variation
  • BIM/BEM translation or preset simulation
  • GIS spatial evidence
  • Python or notebook analysis
  • AI-assisted fact-checking or workflow support
  • spreadsheet comparison or sensitivity

5. Total Recall Board

Submit one visual board using the Total Recall Board template.

The board should show:

  • project image or diagram
  • design decision
  • original evidence
  • weak links
  • missing variable/range/threshold
  • candidate workflow

Assessment Criteria

Criterion Weight
Decision specificity 20%
Honest evidence recall 30%
Quality of critique 25%
Candidate workflow logic 15%
Visual board clarity 10%

Differentiated Expectations

Undergraduate

  • choose a tightly bounded decision
  • use clear evidence categories
  • identify one realistic missing workflow

MArch

  • connect the decision to studio or practice stakes
  • distinguish evidence from argument with sharper judgment
  • identify a workflow that could plausibly change the design

RPg / PhD

  • treat the assignment as a method critique
  • identify transfer limits and uncertainty more explicitly

Common Failure Modes

  • describing the whole project instead of one decision
  • treating precedent images as proof
  • hiding tutor preference or taste behind vague evidence language
  • proposing a workflow without saying what it would output
