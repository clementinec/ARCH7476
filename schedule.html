Course Schedule

12-Week Studio Evidence Workflow

Weekly Schedule Overview

Every week has two parts:

  1. a focused lecture or tool demonstration
  2. a studio-lab where students apply that week’s lens to their own project

Session 2 Format

The usual second-half format is a Project Reasoning Round Table, not a formal presentation. Week 6 includes personal website setup; Week 7 uses the timed A2 walkthrough and prototype clinic described below.

For the usual round-table format, each student uploads one rough material to Slack before Session 2:

  • plan, section, render, massing image, or site photo
  • precedent image or evidence source
  • table, chart, GIS map, GH canvas, BIM/BEM screenshot, notebook output, or prompt log
  • draft object card, presentation slide, or reproducibility note

Selected students speak for 60-90 seconds:

  1. I brought ________.
  2. I chose or questioned this evidence/tool because ________.
  3. It supports ________.
  4. It does not yet show ________.
  5. My question to the room is ________.

Volunteers go first and receive formative participation acknowledgement. A1-A4 grades come from submitted work unless a specific assignment says otherwise. If more examples are needed, the instructor may use random draw or select materials that make the week’s concept visible. The draw is for a short reasoning check-in, not a polished presentation.

At a Glance

Week Lecture / demo lens Session 2 material and check-in Assignment alignment
1 Total Recall: evidence behind a past studio decision past project visual: what decision and evidence are visible? A1 decision + evidence recall
2 Evidence audit: weak links, overclaims, and next decision marked evidence: what does it support, and what does it not show yet? A1 evidence critique
3 Spreadsheet as baseline workflow table/chart/matrix: what variants or trade-offs could be compared? A1 due / A2 decision recap
4 GIS choices for architects site/map/context image: is this decision spatial? A2 evidence map + tool matrix
5 BIM/BEM and performance model translation model/performance material: what assumptions would dominate? A2 tool matrix + feasibility
6 Evidence and pathway mapping workflow choice and A4 branch; personal GitHub Pages setup in half of the lab A2 due / A4 branch
7 A2 to first prototype A2 walkthrough in triads, feedback, one test, and a project-page update A3 prototype scope + first A4 feedback entry
8 Reproducibility, AI fact-checking, and mentor prep prompt log/error/README: what must be fact-checked, relationship-checked, or documented? A3 verification log
9 Thresholds, recommendations, and counter-arguments figure/output/object-card draft: what result changes the action? A3 due / A4 launch + clinic/workgroup vote
10 Draft workflow review draft package: can reviewers trace the full evidence loop? A4 draft clinic or optional workgroup
11 Final package polish final component draft: which A4 component is weakest? A4 near-final clinic or optional workgroup
12 Object Fair final object card/reflection: what reasoning transfers forward? A4 due

Week 1: Total Recall

Lecture / Demo

  • what counts as evidence in studio
  • how design claims get justified after the fact
  • precedent, assumption, intuition, tutor preference, and workflow output

Studio-Lab

Students build a one-page case board from a prior studio decision.

Core prompts:

  • What was one design decision you made?
  • What alternatives existed?
  • What evidence did you use?
  • What did that evidence actually prove?
  • What was assumption or preference?
  • What evidence would have changed the decision?

Session 2 material: upload one image, diagram, or board fragment from the past project.

Round-table check-in: What decision is visible here, and what evidence did you think supported it?

Feeds: A1 Prior Studio Decision + Evidence Recall.

Week 2: Evidence Audit

Lecture / Demo

  • evidence types used in studio
  • weak links, overclaims, and confidence levels
  • what precedent, simulation, survey, and standards can actually support
  • how A1 moves from recollection to diagnosis

Studio-Lab

Students revise their A1 evidence audit and identify what evidence would be needed for the next design decision.

Session 2 material: upload the same decision with evidence, precedent, assumption, and uncertainty marked.

Round-table check-in: What does this evidence actually support, and what does it not show yet?

Feeds: A1 Evidence Critique.

Week 3: Spreadsheet as Baseline Workflow

Lecture / Demo

  • transparent comparison logic
  • evidence logs, decision matrices, and uncertainty ranges
  • chart rescue and the 3-second message test
  • how simple tables can support generative comparison

Studio-Lab

Students turn one A1/A2 decision into a comparison table, chart, or decision matrix.

Minimum lab deliverable:

  • one decision
  • two or more options, variants, or scenarios
  • one comparison structure
  • one output that could make a trade-off visible

Session 2 material: upload a rough table, chart, matrix, or sketch of comparable variants.

Round-table check-in: What variants or scenarios could be compared, and what would make the trade-off visible?

Feeds: A1 final board + A2 Decision Recap.

Week 4: GIS Choices for Architects

Lecture / Demo

  • when a design decision is genuinely spatial
  • QGIS, ArcGIS, and geopandas
  • site, access, boundary, and overlay evidence
  • Hong Kong data sources and setup routes

Studio-Lab

Students decide whether their project needs GIS and, if so, define the smallest useful spatial output.

Session 2 material: upload a site map, plan, context image, route diagram, or spatial layer sketch.

Round-table check-in: Does this decision depend on location, distance, overlap, boundary, or access?

Feeds: A2 Evidence Map + Tool Translation Matrix.

Week 5: BIM, BEM, and Performance Model Translation

Lecture / Demo

  • BIM vs BEM as a model-translation problem
  • how geometry, zoning, construction, and operations change in energy modeling
  • IFC vs gbXML
  • what performance claims need before they are credible

Studio-Lab

Students identify whether their project needs model/performance evidence, a proxy test, or only BIM/BEM literacy.

Session 2 material: upload a BIM/BEM screenshot, section, envelope image, model diagram, or performance claim.

Round-table check-in: What would a performance model need to know, and what would it probably simplify?

Feeds: A2 Tool Translation Matrix + Workflow Feasibility.

Named runnable demo: students inspect the preset simulation kit, run the no-install heat proxy, and see the optional EnergyPlus two-week CLI case. The EnergyPlus case is used when the local installation is ready; the proxy route remains the guaranteed fallback.

Week 6: Evidence and Pathway Mapping

Lecture / Demo

  • evidence source triage
  • pathway choice across spreadsheet, GIS, Python, BIM/BEM, GH, and AI-assisted routes
  • Retrospective Redesign vs Live Studio Application
  • scope discipline: one decision, not the whole project

Studio-Lab

Students submit A2 and declare their provisional A4 branch and workflow plan. Half of this lab was used to set up personal websites with GitHub Pages. Week 7 continues from those sites by adding a project section documenting the A2-to-A3 work.

Session 2 material: upload a workflow sketch, pathway matrix, or A4 branch declaration draft.

Round-table check-in: Which route are you choosing, and what is the smallest useful output?

Feeds: A2 Primary Workflow Selection + A4 Branch Declaration. The personal site provides a place to document subsequent tests and revisions.

Week 7: Prototype Clinic — From A2 to a First Test

Session 1: Demo and First Test (50 minutes)

  • use the submitted A2 to identify one decision, one workflow route, and one small test
  • inspect a worked example: input, what varies, output, and quality check
  • attempt the first test and record a result or the exact step that blocked it
  • prepare the A2 walkthrough and open the project section of the personal site, or a local draft if the site is not ready

Session 2: A2 Walkthrough and Prototype Clinic (50 minutes)

Bring the submitted A2 PDF, the first test result or blocker note, and the personal site or local draft. Every student takes a turn in a group of three. Use pairs for any remainder: two six-minute turns, then six minutes to refine both next-test plans. Brief the speaking and feedback protocol during Session 1’s final five minutes.

Lab time Activity What to do
0-20 min A2 walkthroughs in triads Take 2 minutes to form groups and choose an order. Then take three 6-minute turns: 3 minutes to explain, followed by 3 minutes of feedback from the two peers together.
20-25 min Instructor debrief Discuss recurring issues and identify the changes students should test next.
25-45 min Act on feedback and update the project page Use 10 minutes to revise a step or test the reason for retaining it; save the result or blocker. Use 10 minutes to update the page/local draft with the test and a dated feedback/action entry.
45-50 min Peer page check and Slack exit Use 3 minutes for a page check: 90 seconds per page in pairs, or 60 seconds per page for a remaining group of three. Use the final 2 minutes to post the page URL/local screenshot, test/check result, feedback response, and next action to Slack.

Your 3-minute walkthrough: explain the design decision, the evidence and its limits, why you chose this workflow, and the smallest test you attempted. Show its result or blocker and ask one specific question.

Your peers’ 3-minute feedback: identify one assumption or scope problem and propose one next test. Record which advice you will use, defer, or reject, and why.

Leave with: one attempted test with a result or a documented blocker; a dated feedback/action entry; and an updated project page or local draft showing the decision, workflow, test status, check or limit, and next action.

This is formative feedback on submitted A2 work and preparation for A3. The walkthrough and website are not new graded submissions. A3 remains due at the end of Week 9 and requires at least three variants/scenarios/alternatives and a re-runnable workflow artifact; the Week 7 page records progress toward that submission.

Feeds: A3 Prototype Scope + Workflow Description, and an initial entry for the A4 Feedback + Revision Ledger.

Week 8: Reproducibility, AI Fact-Checking, and Mentor Prep

Lecture / Demo

  • workflow documentation
  • AI as probabilistic companion, not final authority
  • fact-based vs relationship-based AI outputs
  • prompt logs
  • manual checks, calculations, and disclosure
  • mentor consultation preparation

Studio-Lab

Students test whether one workflow component, AI-assisted or not, is documented and checked well enough to be inspected by someone else.

Session 2 material: upload a prompt log, error message, output, README fragment, verification note, or workflow checklist.

Round-table check-in: What must someone else inspect before they trust this output?

Feeds: A3 Verification and Failure Log.

Week 9: Thresholds and Recommendations

Lecture / Demo

  • adopt/defer thresholds
  • uncertainty language
  • counter-arguments
  • decision memo framing

Studio-Lab

Students write a threshold statement and one counter-argument.

Session 2 material: upload a figure, output table, comparison chart, or object-card draft.

Round-table check-in: What result changes the design action, and what uncertainty remains visible?

Feeds: A3 Prototype Output + A4 Practice Case/Object Card.

End-of-Week 9 Class Vote

The instructor checks whether Weeks 10-11 should stay as full-class A4 clinics or split into two modes:

Mode For whom Use of class time
A4 clinic students who need 1-1 help finishing the package desk crits, package triage, reproducibility checks
Optional GH/Python workgroup students with stable tooling who want to build their own pipeline small-group workflow mapping, vibe-coded component review, manual verification
Mixed mode split class interest short shared opening, then clinic/workgroup breakout

The optional workgroup is not a required route. Direct .gh / .ghx starter files are planned for the website and currently WIP; until they are published, the workgroup uses CSV / GeoJSON / Python handoff and student-owned GH setups.

Week 10: Draft Workflow Review

Lecture / Demo

  • how to present a workflow-backed design decision
  • how reviewers inspect decision, evidence, workflow, output, threshold, and limit

Studio-Lab

Default mode is 1-1 or small-panel A4 clinic. If the Week 9 vote shows enough demand, a small optional workgroup may run in parallel for students with stable GH/Python tooling.

No tournament scoring; the emphasis is professional critique and making the final package traceable.

Session 2 material: upload a draft presentation, object card, package outline, or reproducibility capsule index.

Round-table check-in: Can a reviewer trace decision -> variants/scenarios -> workflow -> output -> threshold -> limit?

Feeds: A4 Draft Package.

Optional workgroup output, if it runs:

  • one pipeline sketch
  • one function contract or component brief
  • one manual verification note
  • one decision about whether the workgroup route will actually enter A4

Week 11: Final Package Polish

Lecture / Demo

  • Practice Case
  • Research Brief
  • Object Card
  • Reproducibility Capsule

Studio-Lab

Students run a package audit and complete final revisions. The default expectation is clinic support for A4. If an optional GH/Python workgroup is still useful, it must produce documentation that can fit inside the Reproducibility Capsule rather than becoming a new ungraded side project.

Session 2 material: upload the weakest A4 component or the component most in need of peer review.

Round-table check-in: Which A4 component is weakest, and what must be fixed before submission?

Feeds: A4 Final Package.

The package must show the full loop:

  1. decision
  2. variants or scenarios
  3. workflow
  4. evaluation
  5. threshold
  6. design action
  7. reproducibility check
  8. feedback/revision decision

Week 12: Object Fair

In Class

  • 6-8 minute final presentation
  • 4 minute Q&A
  • object card / evidence board review
  • final reflection on workflow transfer

Session 2 material: use the final object card or reflection note.

Round-table check-in: What design reasoning can transfer to another studio project?

Feeds: A4 Reflection Note.

Final Prompt

What design decision did your workflow improve, what did the evidence change, and what should happen next?
