Week 11: Studio Evidence Package Workshop

Audience Fit, Review, and Final Refinement

Today’s Agenda

  • audience-fit pressure test
  • four review questions, one at a time
  • demonstrated vs. validated: are you overselling?
  • pathway triage for workflows that are not landing
  • package assembly checklist
  • peer feedback triage and revision plan

A4 Has Four Parts

  1. studio decision brief
  2. evidence board / object card
  3. pathway artifact
  4. method appendix

Each part should reinforce the others. If one part contradicts or ignores another, the package reads as unfinished.

The 60-Second Audience Test

A computational pipeline can produce a result without establishing which design response is justified.

Swap your draft package with a partner. They read for 60 seconds, then answer:

  • what decision is at stake?
  • what does the author recommend?
  • how confident should I be?

If they cannot answer all three, the package is not ready for its audience.

Review Question 1: Is the Decision Context Clear?

Good enough: a reader who has never seen your project can name the decision in one sentence after reading the brief.

Common failure: the brief opens with method or background instead of the decision. The reader reaches the recommendation without knowing what was being decided.

Fix: write the decision as the first sentence. Everything else follows from it.

Review Question 2: Does the Evidence Support the Recommendation?

Good enough: each piece of evidence in the package connects to the recommendation through a stated reason, not just proximity on the page.

Common failure: the evidence is real, but the recommendation could be swapped for the opposite and nothing in the package would contradict it.

Fix: for each figure or data point, write one sentence: “this supports the recommendation because ________.” If nothing fills the blank, the evidence is decoration.

Review Question 3: Is the Pathway Visible?

Good enough: a reader can tell which tool produced the evidence and could, in principle, rerun the workflow.

Common failure: the pathway artifact exists but is disconnected from the brief. The spreadsheet, map, or notebook sits in an appendix that no one reads.

Fix: reference the artifact directly in the brief: “the comparison in Figure 2 was produced by ________.”

Review Question 4: Are the Limits Stated Directly?

Good enough: the package names at least one thing the evidence does not establish and one condition under which the recommendation might change.

Common failure: limits appear as a disclaimer paragraph at the end that reads as boilerplate rather than genuine reflection.

Fix: embed limits next to the claims they qualify. “This comparison covers three sites; a fourth with different transit access might change the ranking.”

What Reviewers Should Look For

Spatial/public-space questions:

  • does the map or observation actually answer the claim?
  • could the same spatial data support a different recommendation?

Building/workplace questions:

  • is the performance claim backed by method, not branding?
  • would changing one assumption reverse the conclusion?

Demonstrated vs. Validated

A working pipeline is not a validated claim.

Go through your A4 and mark each claim:

  • demonstrated: “the workflow runs and produces this output”
  • validated: “the output has been checked against independent evidence”

Most student work is demonstrated, not validated. That is fine — but say so. Overselling a demonstration as validation is the fastest way to lose a reviewer’s trust.

Pathway Triage

If your workflow is not producing useful output by Week 11, you have three options:

Option When it fits What changes
Narrow the scope the method works but the question is too broad reduce to one site, one comparison, one variable
Reframe the question the evidence you found answers a different question than you started with rewrite the brief around what you actually have
Pivot the pathway the tool is fighting the question switch to the baseline spreadsheet pathway with what you have collected

Choose one. Do not try all three.

Package Assembly Checklist

Part Contains Rough length
Decision brief decision, evidence summary, recommendation, limits 1–2 pages
Evidence board the visual package: key figures, one dominant message, next action 1 board or 2–3 slides
Pathway artifact the spreadsheet, map, or notebook that produced the evidence included as file or appendix
Method appendix data sources, workflow steps, assumptions, one paragraph on what you would do differently 1 page

If any part is missing, the submission is incomplete.

Peer Feedback Triage

Sort every piece of feedback you received today into three categories:

  • Fix before submission: the reviewer identified something the audience will misread
  • Fix if time allows: a real improvement, but the package works without it
  • Disagree, and can say why: you considered the feedback and have a reason to keep your current approach

Write your triage as a one-page revision plan. Bring it to Week 12.

End-of-Class Goal

Leave with:

  • one completed 60-second audience test (partner’s written answers)
  • every claim in your A4 marked as demonstrated or validated
  • one written revision plan with prioritized fixes
  • a submission-ready package structure, even if content is still being refined
